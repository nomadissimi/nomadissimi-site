# **Chapter 3 Step 1: The 8-Day Mission — Getting Your Receipt (Questura or Poste)**

<GuideBlueNote title="Your 8-day finish line">

Before we talk offices, forms, stamps, or Italian bureaucracy, remember the one result you need from this chapter:

**Within 8 working days of arriving in Italy, you need a dated receipt proving that you moved forward with your first Permesso di Soggiorno application.**

<NavyBadge>Step 1</NavyBadge>

<GuideBadge>8 working days</GuideBadge>

<GuideSpacer size="sm" />

This chapter is slightly different from the usual Permesso advice you may find online.

That is because Italy's Digital Nomad and Remote Worker visas are newer categories, and **the official procedure for your visa category is not the same as the classic postal-kit route used for many older types of residence permits.**

<GuideBigSis title="Why this works differently for you">

Under the interministerial decree of February 29, 2024 governing Italy's Digital Nomad and Remote Worker visa framework, the first Permesso di Soggiorno request for this category is officially initiated **directly through the Questura**.

In other words:

**Your official first stop is the Questura's immigration office, not automatically Poste Italiane.**

However — and welcome to Italy — local implementation is not always perfectly uniform.

Some Questure may process you directly.

Others may still instruct you to use the Poste Italiane kit.

That is why this chapter gives you **both paths**, in the correct order.

</GuideBigSis>

<GuideWarning title="Do not wait until day 7">

Start dealing with this on **day 1 or day 2 after arrival whenever possible.**

Why?

Because if your local Questura tells you to use the postal route instead, you still need enough time to collect the kit, prepare the paperwork, return to a Sportello Amico office, and obtain your dated receipt before your 8-working-day window closes.

**The earlier you start, the more breathing room you have if the local office gives you a different procedure.**

</GuideWarning>

## **What to bring to the Questura**

Do not arrive with just your passport and optimism.

Italian immigration offices are paper-heavy, and being overprepared is infinitely better than being sent home for something you already had sitting in a drawer.

<GuideChecklist title="Your Questura application packet">

- **Your original passport**

- **Your Italian Digital Nomad or Remote Worker visa**

- **Photocopies of your passport identification pages, visa, and relevant entry stamps**

- **Copies of the supporting documents from your consulate application**

  Bring copies of the core documents you used to obtain the visa, including your work or professional documentation, proof of financial resources, health insurance, and accommodation documentation.

- **Your original supporting documents**

  Keep the originals with you in a separate organized folder in case the officer asks to verify them.

- **Proof of your arrival in Italy**

  Bring your boarding pass, flight confirmation, or other documentation showing when you entered Italy.

- **Recent passport-style photographs**

  Bring several recent biometric passport photos so you are not caught short if the office requests them.

- **A €16,00 Marca da Bollo**

  You can buy this official tax stamp at a Tabacchino.

- **Your Codice Fiscale, if you already have one**

  Bring a copy and clearly flag it in your application so another fiscal code is not accidentally generated for you.

- **A printed copy of the official information supporting the direct-Questura procedure for your visa category**

  This is useful if the person at the desk is less familiar with the newer Digital Nomad / Remote Worker permit procedure.

- **A payment method and some cash**

  Government-office payment arrangements vary, and being prepared for more than one payment method is never a bad idea.

</GuideChecklist>

Remember that beautiful folder of paperwork you thought you were finally done with after your visa appointment?

**Bring the same core evidence you used for your visa application, neatly organized and easy to retrieve.**

The Questura is entitled to verify the basis on which your residence permit is being requested.

## **What can happen at the Questura?**

<GuideDecisionBox title="There are two realistic outcomes">

### **Outcome 1: They process the application directly**

Amazing.

The Questura accepts your application under the Digital Nomad / Remote Worker procedure and begins processing your Permesso.

Depending on the office and how they handle first applications, they may also take your fingerprints during the same visit or give you another appointment for fingerprinting.

**Before you leave, make sure you receive written, dated proof that your application was accepted or initiated.**

That is the receipt you are protecting.

---

### **Outcome 2: They tell you to use the Poste Italiane kit**

Do not argue with the clerk for twenty minutes while your 8-day clock keeps ticking.

Politely confirm that they want your application submitted through Poste Italiane, and if possible, ask which permit category or code they want you to use on the postal form.

Then move immediately to **Path B below.**

</GuideDecisionBox>

<GuideBlueNote title="About the fees">

The government fee associated with a standard one-year electronic residence permit remains **€70,46** under the fee structure referenced in this guide.

If your Questura processes the application directly, you are not using Poste Italiane's separate €30,00 mailing service.

If the Questura redirects you to the postal-kit route, the separate Poste Italiane submission fee applies as explained below.

Because government fees can change, always verify current amounts before paying.

</GuideBlueNote>

## **What you need to do**

<GuideSteps title="Your Poste Italiane checklist">

**1. Get the Kit**

Go to a Poste Italiane office with a Sportello Amico and ask:

**"Kit per il permesso di soggiorno."**

**Important:** the postal paperwork may not show a clean, dedicated category for the newer Digital Nomad / Remote Worker permit.

Do **not guess** which category or code should be entered.

If the Questura redirected you to Poste, ask the Questura or the Sportello Amico clerk which category they expect you to use for your application.

## **Prepare the Documents**

Once your forms are complete, place them inside the envelope.

<GuideChecklist title="What goes inside the envelope">

- **Photocopies of your passport**

  At minimum, include the pages containing your identifying information, signature where applicable, Italian visa, and relevant travel stamps.

- **A clear photocopy of the page containing your Italian visa**

- **A copy of your Codice Fiscale, if you already have one**

  If you already have a Codice Fiscale, make that clear in the application.

- **Photocopies in A4 format of the supporting documents used for your visa application**

- **Your €16,00 Marca da Bollo**

## **Returning to the Post Office & The Costs**

Once everything is ready, return to a Poste Italiane office with Sportello Amico and bring the **open envelope**.

<GuideDecisionBox title="Fees">

Inside the kit is a pre-printed payment slip called a **bollettino**.

For the standard one-year permit fee structure used in this guide:

- **€70,46** is paid through the bollettino.

  This consists of the electronic residence-card production cost plus the applicable government contribution.

- **€30,00** is paid separately to Poste Italiane for the postal submission service.

That makes the postal-route total:

**€100,46**

The €16,00 Marca da Bollo is separate and should already have been purchased.

</GuideDecisionBox>

## **The Golden Rule of the Questura: Bring Everything**

The officer at the desk is going to process your application and verify your identity (sometimes even more thoroughly than the consulate appointment).  
Even though you have already submitted photocopies with your application — either in your Yellow Kit or directly at the Questura — the police officer has the right to ask to see the physical originals. Remember when we told you to keep the original stuff you brought to your visa appointment many weeks ago?

<GuideWarning title="Big-sis advice">

Italian clerks and police officers love their paper trails. Treat this appointment exactly like your original consulate visa interview and **bring a well-organized folder containing every single original document you used to get your visa.**

If they ask for a document you don't have with you, don’t panic. The officer will typically issue a formal request giving you 30 days to return and submit the missing paperwork (a process known as _integrazione documenti)_. However, it’s best to be prepared as much as you can to avoid delays.

</GuideWarning>

Here is exactly what you need in your folder:

<GuideChecklist title="What to bring to the Questura">

- **Your application receipt** — the original proof from Step 1, whether it came from Poste Italiane or directly from the Questura. Yes, that super important document we told you not to lose.
- Your **original passport** (The physical booklet).
- 4 identical, recent passport-sized **photos** (45x35 mm). They have to be _different photos_ from what you submitted in your visa application. Take them in Italy ideally to have the most recent pictures and avoid any problems. The photo of your Permesso di Soggiorno should be different from your Italian Visa on your passport.
- **Original Proof of Accommodation:** The registered lease agreement or property deed in your name.
- Original Proof of **Health Insurance:** The full policy certificate showing coverage in Italy, dates, and amounts.
- **Original Job Documents:** Your work contract, recent payslips, or your client invoices.  
  _This is further proof of your annual income._
- Your **Flight** Reservation and Boarding Pass: Keep the physical stub or a printed PDF of the ticket you used to fly into Italy. It proves your exact date of entry.

</GuideChecklist>

## **How to check your status**

<GuideBlueNote title="Track it online">

You don't have to guess when your card is ready and make a bunch of trips to the ugly Questura to ask. You can simply track its progress online (yay).

## **The Big Reveal**

<GuideSoftPurple title="Your Permesso di Soggiorno">

When you finally hold that hard plastic card in your hand, take a moment to realize what you’ve accomplished. **This is your official Permesso di Soggiorno (residence card)\!**

</GuideSoftPurple>

<GuideUnlockCard>

Your Codice Fiscale is finally printed on a document! By applying for a permesso di soggiorno for the first time, the Italian authorities generate your codice fiscale.

Look closely at the front of your new card: you will see a 16-character alphanumeric string printed on it. That is your official **Codice Fiscale\!** _This code is your absolute lifeline in Italy and will be the same, even after you renew your Permesso di Soggiorno in the future._

</GuideUnlockCard>

<GuideWarning title="Very important distinction">

However, please be careful: your Codice Fiscale is your personal identification code; it is NOT a business tax number (_Partita IVA_). The fiscal code is different from a VAT number.  
You cannot use it to invoice clients as a freelancer. But you can use it to access the Italian healthcare system, sign up for home internet, and even get tax deductions at the pharmacy.

</GuideWarning>

# **Chapter 6 Step 4: The Comune Residency Registration (making your address official)**

Now that you have your physical _Permesso di Soggiorno_, it is time to tell the Italian government exactly where you live.

<NavyBadge>Comune registration</NavyBadge>
<GuideBadge>Make your address official</GuideBadge>

<GuideSpacer size="sm" />

You need to officially register your residency (_Iscrizione Anagrafica_) at the local _Anagrafe_ (the registry office of your town hall, or Comune).

## **The Digital Identity Hurdle**

<GuideBlueNote title="Why this part feels paradoxical">

Italy has a centralized national portal for residency registration called the _ANPR_, and many cities also have their own specific local online portals.  
However, to log into any of these online portals, you need an Italian digital identity like SPID or CIE (electronic Italian ID card).

As a newcomer, you usually do not have SPID, and you do not have the CIE yet (that is literally Step 5). **In fact, you need to register your residency precisely so you can get the CIE\!**

Remember that the CIE is the _Carta d’Identità Elettronica_ (Italian ID card), which is different from your _Permesso di Soggiorno_ (residency card).

</GuideBlueNote>

Because of this, as a first-time foreign registrant, you have three different routes to get around the digital block:

<GuideChecklist title="Your 3 possible routes">

- **Route 1: The Remote Email Route**  
  Many Comunes allow foreigners without a digital identity to bypass the portal and submit their residency applications via standard email. Go to your specific Comune’s website, search for “Iscrizione anagrafica stranieri”, and look for the specific email address. You simply download the PDF form, fill it out, attach your documents, and email it.

- **Route 2: The “Roommate” SPID Route**  
  If you are moving in with a partner, family member, friend, or roommate who already has an active Italian SPID or CIE, they can usually log into the portal and submit the joint residency registration on behalf of the entire household. You can also try to use the SPID/CIE of someone not living with you, but more likely, they can ask you for further documentation or reject it.

- **Route 3: The In-Person Route**  
  If your Comune doesn't allow email submissions and you live alone, you must go physically to the Ufficio Anagrafe. Do not assume they are open 9-to-5, Monday through Friday. Italian offices have weird schedules. Check their specific opening hours and book an appointment online if possible. If the clerk asks you to “just do it online with the SPID,” calmly explain that you are an extra-EU citizen doing your first registration and you do NOT have a digital identity yet.

</GuideChecklist>

## **What Documents Do You Need?**

Whether you do this via email or in person, the Anagrafe will want to see a very specific set of documents to prove you have the legal right to live in that apartment. Have these ready:

<GuideChecklist title="Comune registration packet">

- Your **Permesso di Soggiorno** (The physical card you just picked up).  
  _The codice fiscale is written here._

- Your **Passport**.

- The **Registered Lease Agreement**: You cannot just show a signed piece of paper. Please keep in mind that **the lease must be officially registered with the _Agenzia delle Entrate_ (Italian Revenue Agency).** You need to bring that part as well. If you have purchased a home, you will need to provide the deed of sale (_Rogito_).

- The **Residency Declaration Form**: Filled out and signed.  
  _Crucial detail:_ **Always provide a working cell phone number** (**preferably Italian**) on this form. And keep an eye out on your SMS notifications.

- The **TARI Declaration Form**: (See below).

</GuideChecklist>

## **Understanding the TARI Form**

<GuideOfficeCard office="TARI" title="What is the TARI?">

When you register your residency, you usually also have to register for the _TARI_.  
**TARI is the Italian garbage tax**, and it is paid by whoever actually lives in the property _(yes, even if you are just renting)_. TARI stands for _Tassa sui Rifiuti_ (waste tax), which is the Italian waste disposal or garbage tax introduced in 2014.

To fill this out, the _Comune_ will ask for the _Dati Catastali_ (cadastral data) of your apartment. This is the government's official ID code for the physical building.

</GuideOfficeCard>

<GuideQuickFacts title="How to find the dati catastali">

_How do you find it?_ Look at your rental contract.  
By law, the _dati catastali_ (usually broken down into _Foglio, Particella, and Subalterno_) must be written inside your lease. If you can't find it, ask your landlord for a copy of their old TARI receipt: the numbers are printed right there.

</GuideQuickFacts>

## **The 45-Day Police Check (no need to stress\!)**

Once you submit your paperwork, your residency registration at the comune is not instantly approved. By law, the local police _(Vigili Urbani)_ have 45 business days to physically visit your apartment to verify that you actually live there.

<GuideBigSis title="How to pass the police check smoothly">

_Big-Sis Tips:_

- **Label your intercom:** Put your last name on the doorbell and mailbox immediately (even if you’re renting). You can literally take a sticker and write your last name next to the landlord’s last name on the intercom. If the police don't see your identifiable last name, they will assume you don't live there and fail your application.

- **Make friends with the neighbors:** Introduce yourself to your building's porter (_portiere_) if you have one, the building administrator, or your immediate neighbors. If the police show up while you are grocery shopping, they will often ask the neighbors, _“Do you know your new neighbor?”_

- **Answer your phone:** They usually drop by unannounced.  
  But if they miss you (they are allowed to try up to three times), they will likely call the phone number you put on the form.  
  Just answer the phone, explain you are at work or out running errands, and tell them when you will be home. _Surprisingly, they are usually very reasonable\!_

</GuideBigSis>

<GuideSoftPurple title="What happens after the check">

The Comune will formally confirm your registration a few days after the officer visits you and verifies that you live there.

_Good news:_ Step 4 is free (the Comune does not charge a fee to submit your residency registration request).

</GuideSoftPurple>

<GuideBigSis title="Final boss next">

You are now officially a registered resident of Italy with a fully verified address. You are done with step 4 out of 5. Now you just need your CIE (Italian ID card).

</GuideBigSis>

# **Chapter 7 Step 5: Your Italian ID Card**

This is the last step of your administrative setup as an Italian resident. It's time to get your official Italian ID card, your ultimate access key!

<NavyBadge>Step 5</NavyBadge>
<GuideBadge>CIE</GuideBadge>

<GuideSpacer size="sm" />

More importantly, the Italian ID card unlocks your digital identification for almost every public service in the country through the "Entra con CIE" system, which we will cover below.

<GuideBlueNote title="Why this step comes last">

You must request it through your local Comune, but they will only let you do this _after_ your residency has been officially registered and the police officer has verified your address. _This is exactly why Step 4 had to happen first._

</GuideBlueNote>

Every city handles appointments a bit differently. Some use a national booking portal, while others have their own local system.  
Your best move is to **search your specific Comune's website for _“Carta d’identità elettronica” + “prenotazione”_ to see how they want you to book.** However, the process is quite similar among the different local offices.

## **What to bring to the appointment**

Most Comuni will ask for a simple set of documents. Bring the following:

<GuideChecklist title="CIE appointment packet">

- **Your passport**
- **Your Permesso di Soggiorno** (your physical card).
- Your **Codice Fiscale**.  
  _Big-Sis reminder:_ Your official Codice Fiscale is already printed right on the front of your Permesso di Soggiorno.  
  If the clerk asks to see your "_Tessera Sanitaria_" to verify your tax code (because Italians are used to presenting their health card for this), just calmly point to the code printed on your residence permit.
- **Two recent printed passport-style photos** that meet the official biometric standards. Make sure these photos are different from the ones you used at your visa appointment and your permesso di soggiorno application since it will be for a different (yet super important) identity document.

</GuideChecklist>

## **Your CIE codes**

<GuideUnlockCard title="PIN + PUK = gold">

When you apply at the counter, the clerk will give you security codes called **PIN** and **PUK**.  
For security reasons, **these arrive split into two halves.**

The first half is given to you right away on a paper receipt at the Comune.  
The second half will arrive a few days later, hidden inside the sealed envelope that contains your physical ID card.

**Treat these codes like gold.** Keep the paper receipt, scan it, laminate it, and save the numbers in a secure password manager. You need both halves combined to use the digital features of the card. They serve as an authentication.

</GuideUnlockCard>

<GuideBigSis title="Pro tip: set up the CieID app immediately">

**Pro tip: set up the CieID app as soon as possible**  
Once you have the physical card and the full PIN, you can register the electronic version (Carta di Identità Elettronica, also known as CIE) on the official _CieID app_. _This turns your smartphone into a digital key._  
You simply hold the card to the back of your phone (using NFC), type in your PIN, and you can instantly log into government websites without needing a separate SPID.

</GuideBigSis>

## **Wait, do I still need a SPID?**

<GuideDecisionBox title="CIE vs SPID">

If you have spent any time on online forums, you have probably seen people stressing out about getting their _SPID,_ which stands for _Sistema Pubblico di Identità Digitale_ (public system of digital identity).

The good news is that you can basically ignore it\! SPID and CIE do the exact same job.  
They are both simply digital keys used to log into Italian government portals.

**Once you have your physical CIE card and the CieID app set up, you have the highest level of digital access to the system.** Once you have your CIE, you do not need to jump through extra hoops or pay third-party providers to get a SPID. Your CIE is all you will need\!

</GuideDecisionBox>
